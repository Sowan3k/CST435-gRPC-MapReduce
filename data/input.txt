The harbour town woke slowly that morning. Fishing boats rocked against the pier, and the first bakery lights flickered on along the main street. A thin mist hung over the water and hid the far end of the breakwater.

Maya carried a wooden crate of oranges from the market to her aunt's stall. The crate was heavier than it looked, so she stopped twice to rest her arms. Each time, a different stranger nodded as if they understood.

By noon the sun had burned off the mist. Children ran along the sea wall while their parents argued cheerfully about the price of fish. A street musician tuned an old guitar and played the same four notes until someone gave him a coin.

In the afternoon a storm gathered quickly over the hills. The vendors covered their goods with blue tarpaulins and moved their tables under the awning of the post office. Rain drummed on the roof, and for a while nobody spoke.

When the rain stopped, the whole town smelled of wet stone and fresh bread. Maya counted the money in her apron and found that the day had been better than expected. She bought a warm loaf for her aunt and one for herself.

That evening the boats returned one by one with their lamps lit. The fishermen unloaded their nets and told the same stories they told every night. Maya listened from the pier until the last light went out.
